About this project

What this shows

Each marker is one HDB block. Its colour is the time since a food distribution was last recorded there, so blocks that have gone longest without one stand out first. Selecting a food type narrows the map to blocks that received it, which shows not only who is served but what they are served.

The panel listing two organisations within seven days is the duplication question stated directly: the same block visited twice in a week by different providers.

How the data is collected

Volunteers record the same six things after every distribution: where it happened, which organisation ran it, the date, what food was given, and anything they noticed. Every record arrives through a single Google Form, so the team and partner organisations all submit in the same format.

No information about recipients is collected or stored. Records are kept at block level only, which keeps the project clear of privacy obligations and makes partner organisations more willing to let their distributions be recorded.

What it cannot tell you

A block marked never recorded means no record exists, not that nobody serves it. The team can only witness the distributions it attends, so absence on this map is absence of evidence.

Quantities are not recorded, so this cannot measure how much food reached a block. The two and six week thresholds are a working assumption and should be revised once the real cadence in Bedok is known.

Every record shown here was submitted by the project team. Reference data (blocks and organisations) is managed in the Supabase Table Editor; distributions are logged through the Google Form linked above.

To connect a real database, set SUPABASE_URL and SUPABASE_ANON_KEY at the top of this file's script. See README.md.
